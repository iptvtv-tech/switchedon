It's the Government's app for keeping digital versions of official documents on your phone and proving who you are, online or in person. You can't get it yet: its pilot passed 10,000 sign-ups and is closed to new people, and the Government says a wider public release is coming.
{: .answer}

When it's released, using it will be voluntary, and you'll need a verified MyGovID to set it up. EU law says these wallets must be free to get and use.
{: .answer}

## When you can get it

The pilot closed to new sign-ups after passing its target of 10,000. The Department of Public Expenditure says it's using what it learned to refine the app before a wider public release, and it posts updates on gov.ie/DigitalWallet. It hasn't given a date.

EU law requires Ireland to make a digital wallet available by the end of 2026, and public bodies are due to start accepting these wallets by then. Banks and payment providers are due to follow by the end of 2027.

The pilot app has no Irish language option. The Government says the final version will have a full Irish language option.

## What it will hold and do

The Government says it will let you keep digital versions of your birth cert, driving licence, European health card and other official documents on your phone. Which documents it holds when it launches depends on new Irish laws that are still being prepared.

gov.ie's demonstrations show three uses:

- **Filling in an online form:** instead of uploading documents, you share your name, date of birth and PPS number from the app, after seeing what's asked for and agreeing.
- **Proving you're over 18 online,** without sharing your name, date of birth or address.
- **Proving you're over 18 in person,** at a cinema for example: you scan a QR code on the staff member's device and share that you're over 18, with your photo so they can compare it with you.

The Government also says the wallet will support the age checks some online platforms need under the Online Safety Code. For more on those checks, see [Why am I being asked to verify my age online?]({{ '/apps/age-verification-online/' | relative_url }})

Looking for a way to pay with your phone instead? See [Which Irish banks support Apple Pay and Google Pay?]({{ '/apps/apple-pay-google-pay-banks/' | relative_url }})

## What you'll need

- **A verified MyGovID account.** See [Setting up a verified MyGovID on your phone]({{ '/apps/mygovid-phone/' | relative_url }}).
- **A phone.** The wallet and your documents are stored on your phone, and you open it with a 6-digit PIN, or with Face ID, Touch ID or your fingerprint. gov.ie doesn't yet say which phones the public version will work on.

## Do you have to use it?

No. EU law says using these wallets is voluntary, and that you mustn't be refused services or put at a disadvantage for not using one: you can still use the existing ways of proving who you are. The Government calls its approach "digital first, not digital only".

## Privacy and security

The Government says:

- your data is stored on your phone, and the Government won't track or record what's in the wallet
- you see exactly what an organisation is asking for, and nothing is shared until you agree
- for some checks, such as being over 18, the wallet can share just a yes or no
- the organisation doing the check doesn't keep a copy of your data
- organisations that want to accept the wallet will have to register and say what data they need, and can ask only for that
- each document in the wallet is digitally signed, so one that's been changed stops being valid

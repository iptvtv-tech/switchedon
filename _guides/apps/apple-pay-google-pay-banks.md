All the main ones do. AIB, Bank of Ireland, PTSB, An Post Money and credit union current accounts all let you add your card to Apple Pay and Google Pay, though some business credit cards are left out.
{: .answer}

If your bank isn't here, check its own website: Google's Irish list leaves out some banks that do support Google Pay.
{: .answer}

## Which cards work

|  | Apple Pay | Google Pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AIB | All AIB cards except Visa Corporate and Business credit cards | The same |
| Bank of Ireland | Debit cards (sole, joint and business) and personal credit cards | Debit cards and personal credit cards |
| PTSB | Visa debit and credit cards | Personal debit and credit cards, and business debit cards |
| An Post Money | Current account debit card and credit card | Current account debit card and credit card |
| Credit union current accounts | Current account debit card | Current account debit card |

PTSB says your card must be activated before you add it.

Apple's and Google's own Irish lists include many more card providers, such as Revolut, N26, bunq and Avant Money. Google's list leaves out Bank of Ireland, PTSB and An Post, even though each says on its own website that Google Pay works with its cards.

## What you need

- **Apple Pay:** an iPhone, Apple Watch or iPad.
- **Google Pay:** an Android phone with NFC (the chip that lets it pay contactlessly), using the Google Wallet app.

## Adding your card

You can usually add your card from your bank's own app. In AIB's app, go to Cards and tap "Add to Apple Wallet"; in PTSB's app, go to "Manage Cards and PINs".

On an iPhone you can also open the Wallet app, tap +, and scan your card or type in its details.

## Paying more than €50

Paying with your phone or watch isn't held to the €50 contactless limit that applies to cards, according to Bank of Ireland, PTSB and An Post Money. But AIB and Bank of Ireland both say some shops' card machines may not accept a phone payment over €50.

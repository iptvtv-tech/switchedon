Yes, the Uber app works in Ireland, but only as a way to book a licensed taxi. Irish rules mean Uber can only act as a booking agent for licensed taxi drivers, so you won't get the ride-sharing service it runs in many other countries.
{: .answer}

You pay the regulated taxi fare on the meter plus a €3 booking fee, or a fixed price Uber shows before you book. Either way, the driver has a taxi licence you can check.
{: .answer}

## What you get

Every taxi ride you book with Uber is with a fully licensed taxi driver. Uber's Irish site has taxi pages for Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick, Drogheda, Tralee, Sligo, Ennis, Waterford and Killarney, and for Dublin and Shannon airports.

In Dublin, Uber says you can request a taxi 24/7, including for airport trips, and larger taxis take up to 6 people.

## What it costs

{% include price-date.html what="Fares and fees" note="They can change, so check Transport for Ireland's and Uber's own pages." %}

Taxi fares are regulated by the National Transport Authority and worked out by the taximeter. Without a written agreement in advance, a driver can't charge more than the meter shows.

Booking by app adds a €3 booking fee to the meter. Each extra adult adds €1; one child travels free, and every two more children under 12 add €1. Tolls are added as you pass them.

Uber also offers Taxi Fixed Price. The price you see before you book is the most you'll pay. If the meter comes in lower, you pay the metered fare plus a €1 Uber technology fee. Changing your destination, adding a stop or changing the route changes the price.

App companies can charge their own fees on top of the meter, so the app receipt may differ from the taximeter receipt. Transport for Ireland's advice is to shop around. To estimate a fare before you go, use its Taxi Fare Estimator; the actual fare may vary with traffic, timing and route.

## Paying

In the app, you pay with a card or PayPal account added to your Uber account, so you don't need cash. You can still give the driver a cash tip.

Since 1 September 2022, every taxi has had to accept cards as well as cash, with no extra charge for paying by card.

## Checking your driver

- Use the National Transport Authority's Driver Check app to check that the driver and car are licensed.
- The driver must display an ID card and a Passenger Information Card where you can see them.
- Drivers must offer you the receipt from the taximeter. The licence number on it should match the number on the taxi's roof sign.
- In the Uber app, you can share your trip details, and the Emergency Button can call emergency services. The emergency number is 112.

## If something goes wrong

To complain about a taxi, use the complaint form on the Transport for Ireland website. Complaints can't be anonymous, and anything criminal should go to the Gardaí.

If you leave something behind and booked through a booking service such as an app, contact it as soon as possible. Drivers who can't return an item usually hand it in at the nearest Garda station. Having the taxi's licence number helps the Gardaí find it.

## Other taxi apps

Uber isn't the only taxi app here. FREENOW, which has an Irish office in Dublin, lets you book a taxi in its app and pay without cash. Bolt added taxi trips in 21 more counties in February 2026. Each app can charge its own fees, so Transport for Ireland suggests shopping around.

Yes: keeping your mobile number when you switch is free, and your new network handles it, so just tell it you want to keep your number when you sign up.
{: .answer}

Your old contract ends automatically when the new service starts, and any loss of service shouldn't last more than one working day.
{: .answer}

{% include key-points.html %}

## Before you switch

**Check whether you're still in contract.** ComReg says bill pay contracts can run for up to 24 months. If you leave early, your contract sets out any early termination fee, usually based on the months left and the monthly cost of your plan. On prepay you aren't tied into a fixed term. SIM-only plans can be rolling or fixed-term, so check yours.

**Check your phone is unlocked.** A locked phone can only be used with the network it was sold with. At the end of a fixed-term contract, your network will unlock it free when you ask for an unlock code. That can take a few days, so ask before your switching date. If you're still in contract, you may need to pay off the rest of the phone first.

**Check the coverage.** ComReg suggests making sure the new network has coverage at home, at work and in places you visit often. Use ComReg's Coverage Map to check.

**Have these ready,** as ComReg lists them: your current contract details, your current plan or bundle details, your bank or payment details, your mobile number, and your address or Eircode.

## How the switch works

1. Contact the new network and tell it you want to keep your number. It isn't done automatically.
2. The new network contacts your old one and manages the switch, so you don't have to go between them.
3. You and the new network agree the date for the switch and the number transfer. If there's a delay, you must be kept updated.
4. Follow the new network's steps. These vary: Tesco Mobile, for example, texts a verification code to the SIM from your current network, so keep that SIM in a phone that can receive texts until the move is done.
5. Your number must be working on the new network within one working day of the agreed date, and any loss of service mustn't last more than one working day. If the move fails, your old network must switch your number and services back on.

Your old contract ends when your new service begins, so there should be no overlap or double billing.

Moving to an eSIM at the same time? See [Switching to eSIM in Ireland: what to check first]({{ '/phones/switch-to-esim/' | relative_url }}).

## Credit, contacts and your old account

- **Credit:** Tesco Mobile says credit can't be transferred with your number, but you can ask your old network to refund unused prepay credit, or credit on a bill pay account. It may charge an administration fee, which should be fair and match what the refund costs it.
- **Contacts:** Tesco Mobile advises copying your contacts to your new SIM, as they won't transfer automatically.
- **Already left?** You can still move your number for at least a month after your contract ends, or longer if your old network agrees, unless you gave up that right.

## If something goes wrong

Networks must compensate you for problems such as a loss of service, a delay in switching, a delay in ending your old service, or not keeping you informed. Each network sets its own compensation and publishes its scheme, and how to claim, on its website.

To complain:

1. Complain to the network under its code of practice, and get a complaint reference number.
2. The network must send you a response within 10 working days.
3. If it doesn't acknowledge your complaint or respond in time, or you're unhappy with its answer, ComReg's Consumer Care team may take it up with the network for you: call (01) 804 9668 (Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm, Saturday 9am to 1pm) or email consumerline@comreg.ie.

Your complaint to the network must have been made within the previous 12 months.

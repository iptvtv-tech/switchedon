'Likely Scam' is a warning label, not proof that a text is a scam. Since 3 July 2025, a text sent under a name that isn't on ComReg's SMS Sender ID Registry shows 'Likely Scam' as the sender, so treat it with care and don't click any link or call any number in it.
{: .answer}

Some genuine organisations' texts get the label too, because they haven't registered their name yet, or a new registration hasn't been switched on by every SMS provider.
{: .answer}

## What the label means

ComReg, the communications regulator, set up the SMS Sender ID Registry. A Sender ID is the name a text comes from, such as 234BANK. Sender IDs include letters or other permitted characters, which is what makes them different from phone numbers.

Texts from registered Sender IDs arrive as normal. Texts from Sender IDs that aren't registered show 'Likely Scam' as the sender. This started on 3 July 2025, to warn people about possible text scams.

## Why a genuine text can say 'Likely Scam'

ComReg says a 'Likely Scam' text may be from a genuine business or organisation that hasn't registered its Sender ID yet, or it could be a real scam.

Registering doesn't always remove the label straight away. A newly registered name may still show as 'Likely Scam' until every SMS provider has switched it on, and ComReg says some names that are already registered are getting the label too.

If you're expecting a text from an organisation and a 'Likely Scam' one arrives, ComReg advises checking it, because the organisation may not have registered yet. Contact the organisation using details you find yourself, on its official website or app or with a web search, not the details in the text.

## What to do with a 'Likely Scam' text

- Be cautious of what it says, and don't rush to respond if it seems urgent.
- Don't click on any links in it.
- Don't call any numbers in it.
- Never give your bank details, card details, PPS number or passwords.
- To check whether it's genuine, contact the organisation using details from its official website or app, or find them with a web search.
- ComReg points to FraudSMART's scam checker at ScamChecker.ie, where you can enter a website link to check it for risks.

## If you clicked a link or think you've been scammed

If you clicked a link, close the web page and the message straight away.

If you suspect fraud, contact your bank and report it to your local Garda station straight away. ComReg also says to contact your local Garda station or visit [garda.ie](https://www.garda.ie/) if you've seen a suspected scam.

## Texts without the label can still be scams

The register only covers Sender IDs, the names with letters in them. Scam texts can also come from ordinary phone numbers: ComReg has warned about texts from an Irish mobile number, with a link, pretending to be from an Irish bank. Be wary of a text from a number you don't know, even if it looks like an Irish number.

## Will these texts be blocked?

Not yet. ComReg planned to block texts from unregistered Sender IDs from 3 October 2025, but put that off until the remaining technical problems are fixed. Until then, the 'Likely Scam' label stays. ComReg's page, updated on 26 November 2026, still says this.

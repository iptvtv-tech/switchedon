It depends on your network. Since Brexit the EU's 'Roam like at home' rules no longer cover the UK, so Irish networks don't have to include it: Vodafone and Three include the UK with their EU roaming, and eir's pages don't say either way.
{: .answer}

On any other network, check its roaming page or app before you go.
{: .answer}

## Why the UK is different now

'Roam like at home' is the EU's roaming policy, in place since 2017. Since Brexit it no longer applies in the UK. Some Irish networks have chosen to include the UK anyway, but ComReg, the communications regulator, says they're not obliged to.

You still have some protections in the UK. Your network must tell you about roaming charges you could run up, alert you when charges start or when you reach the data roaming cap of €61.50 (including VAT), tell you how to avoid roaming by accident, and tell you if your contract terms change.

## What Vodafone, Three and eir say

- **Vodafone** says you can use your phone in Europe or the UK just like at home, at no extra cost. Bill pay customers use their home data allowance; on pay as you go it's your home allowance, or 45GB of roaming data on unlimited plans. Some plans have a fair usage data limit in Europe.
- **Three**'s price guide says roaming in the UK and EU is included in its plans, and its UK covers the Channel Islands, Gibraltar and the Isle of Man. Its online rate checker shows your EU data allowance applying in the UK and in Northern Ireland, with incoming calls free.
- **eir**'s bill pay and prepay roaming pages, and its prepay price list, don't name the UK in any roaming band. Ask eir before you go.

Other networks choose their own UK terms too. Check your network's roaming page or app, or ask it, before you travel.

## Before you go

- Check your plan's roaming allowance on your network's website or app.
- When you arrive, you should get a text about any limits or charges that apply.

## Near the border and in Northern Ireland

Near the border, your phone can connect to a network on the other side without you noticing. ComReg says that in Northern Ireland you may be charged for cross-border roaming, depending on your network.

To stop your phone switching network by itself, turn off automatic network selection in its settings and choose your network yourself.

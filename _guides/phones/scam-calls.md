## How to spot a scam call

ComReg's advice is to Stay Scam Safe:

- **Stop:** don't rush. Hang up straight away if a caller pressures you, says you need to act urgently, or threatens you with consequences.
- **Check:** do you recognise the number?
- **Avoid:** if you're unsure, don't use any number or link the caller gives you. Contact the organisation using details from its official website, app or another trusted source.
- **Mark it:** mark the number as spam, then block it if it keeps calling you.

A number on your screen proves nothing. Scammers can fake the number that shows, so a call can look as if it comes from a genuine organisation. As the Gardaí put it, just because a number looks Irish doesn't mean it is. Scammers can also find your basic details online, so don't trust a caller just because they know some of your details.

The common types ComReg sees in Ireland:

- **Faked numbers:** a number made to look like a genuine one.
- **Wangiri:** a short call or missed call to get you to ring back an international number, which makes money for the scammer.
- **Vishing:** a call, which may sound robotic, trying to get your personal and bank details, account numbers or passwords.
- **Tech support calls:** someone offering technical support, to get you to log into your bank account.
- **Robocalls:** a recorded message with options that put you through to the scammer.

## What never to do

- Never give personal information to a caller: bank details, your PPS number, card details, your name and address, passport numbers or passwords.
- Never share your card PIN or online banking password, and never transfer money because a caller asks you to. The Gardaí say your bank will never ask you to do either.
- Don't follow instructions from a recorded message.
- Don't call back numbers you don't recognise, and be wary of several calls or missed calls from the same unfamiliar number, especially one that looks like your own.
- Don't download software or programs you don't recognise.

If a caller says they're from your bank, the Gardaí suggest taking their number, saying you'll call back, and then ringing your bank on a number you look up yourself. If you think it was a bogus call, report it to your bank.

## Getting a number blocked

If the same number keeps ringing you with scam calls, ComReg says to contact your network and ask for that number to be blocked. You can also mark it as spam and block it on your phone.

ComReg also suggests saving the numbers of friends and family abroad in your phone, with the country code.

## Why you still get scam calls

ComReg and the networks have had four measures in place since October 2024. They block calls that use numbers organisations never call out from, numbers not yet given to any network, and calls from abroad faking Irish landline or mobile numbers. ComReg says these blocked over 131 million scam calls between February 2023 and October 2025.

Some still get through, because it's hard to block scammers quickly without blocking genuine numbers too. Scammers use software to ring many numbers at once and note which ones answer, and those numbers can be sold on to other criminals. ComReg says going ex-directory or joining the "do not call" register for marketing won't stop scam calls.

## If you've been caught out

ComReg says there's no need to feel embarrassed: scammers have become very sophisticated. The Gardaí's advice is to act quickly:

1. Change your passwords and PINs.
2. Contact your bank and ask it to recall any payment.
3. Report it to your local Garda station, bringing copies of any details you have.

Be careful afterwards: ComReg warns that people who have been scammed may be contacted by other scammers offering to get their money back for a fee.

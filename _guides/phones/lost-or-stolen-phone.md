If your phone is lost or stolen, contact your mobile network straight away so it can block your SIM and, if you give it the phone's IMEI number, bar the phone from working on any mobile network in Ireland. Report it to the Gardaí as well.
{: .answer}

If Find My (iPhone) or Find Hub (Android) was turned on, you can also try to find the phone, lock it and, as a last resort, erase it from another device or a web browser.
{: .answer}

## 1. Try to find it and lock it

**iPhone.** Use the Find My app on another Apple device or on a Family Sharing member's device, or sign in at icloud.com/find. You don't need a verification code to sign in there, so it works even if the missing phone is the one that gets your codes. You can play a sound, mark the phone as lost, or erase it.

Marking it as lost turns on Lost Mode: the phone is locked with your passcode, Apple Pay cards and passes are suspended, and you can show a phone number and a message on its screen. It only shows up if it's switched on and connected to Wi-Fi or mobile data.

**Android.** Use the Find Hub app on another Android phone or tablet, Find Hub in a web browser, or a friend's device as a guest. Play sound rings the phone at full volume for five minutes, even if it's on silent. Mark as lost locks it with your PIN, pattern or password, and you can add contact details and a message to the lock screen.

Find Hub turns on automatically when you add a Google Account. To lock or erase the phone, it needs power, a mobile data or Wi-Fi connection, your Google Account signed in, Find Hub turned on, and to be visible on Google Play.

If Find My or Find Hub wasn't on before the phone went missing, you can't use it to find, lock or erase the phone. Go straight to your network.

## 2. Call your mobile network

ComReg's advice is to contact your mobile provider immediately, with your IMEI number. Thieves can quickly run up big bills, and you may be responsible for them if you don't act quickly. Your network can block your SIM and, with the IMEI, bar the phone itself.

- **Vodafone:** chat, call 1907 from a Vodafone mobile, or 1800 225588 from any other phone, and it will block the phone and SIM.
- **Three:** customer care on 1913, or +353 83 333 3333 from abroad.
- **eir:** call 1901 or go to one of its 71 stores. It can give you a replacement SIM, in store or by post, and blacklist the phone.
- **Virgin Media:** freephone 1908 or online chat. You can also cancel your SIM in My Virgin Media.
- **Tesco Mobile:** call 1903, 1749 or 089 420 0000 from Ireland, or chat, during opening hours. From abroad, call 1743 from a Tesco Mobile phone or +353 89 420 0000 from any other phone. Out of hours, leave a voicemail on those numbers, send a message in the app or on the website, or use the contact form.

On another network? The Gardaí's advice is the same: contact your service provider to have the IMEI blocked.

Your network can give you a replacement SIM. eir can post one or give you one in a store. Tesco Mobile can post one, or you can pick one up in your nearest Tesco store, and it moves your number and credit across.

## 3. Get the phone barred with its IMEI

The IMEI is a 15-digit number that identifies your phone. Give it to your network and it can register the phone on a central barring database, the CEIR. The phone then won't work on any mobile network in Ireland, even with another SIM in it.

To find the IMEI:

- **On the phone itself:** dial \*#06#.
- **Android phone you've lost:** in the Find Hub app, select the phone, then Settings. Or go to android.com/find and select Settings next to the phone.

Apple says the police may ask for your iPhone's serial number, and that you can find it even if you don't have the phone.

## 4. Report it to the Gardaí

An Garda Síochána's advice is to report a lost or stolen phone to them straight away, as well as contacting your network. Vodafone also tells customers to report a loss or theft to the Gardaí, or the local police if abroad, and to get a PULSE (crime reference) number.

If the phone was stolen in Ireland (not Northern Ireland), you can use the Garda Online Crime Declaration. Don't use it if the phone was taken by force or threat of force, in a burglary of your home or business, or in any violent crime: contact your local Garda station, or call 999 or 112 in an emergency.

Declare a theft only once. Print the details before you submit, because you can't view, change or print the declaration afterwards.

## 5. Erase it if you can't get it back

**iPhone.** You can erase it from Find My or icloud.com/find. Erasing can't be undone, so try everything else first. If the phone is offline, the erase starts the next time it's online. Don't remove it from Find My afterwards: that removes Activation Lock, which makes it easier for a thief to unlock and resell the phone.

**Android.** In Find Hub, select the phone, then Settings, then Factory reset. This permanently deletes everything on the phone (it may not delete an SD card), and the phone then no longer shows in Find Hub. If you get it back later, you'll need your Google Account password to use it.

## 6. Protect your accounts and watch for scams

Check your Apple Account on another trusted device or at account.apple.com. If you see changes you didn't make, correct them and change your password. For an Android phone you can't find, Google's advice is to secure your Google Account.

Apple will never contact you to say your iPhone has been found. Never share your passcode, passwords or verification codes with anyone who asks, and watch out for phishing messages and fake support calls. For scam texts, see [What does 'Likely Scam' mean on a text message?]({{ '/phones/likely-scam-texts/' | relative_url }}).

## Before it happens

- **Note your IMEI:** dial \*#06# and keep the number safe. Vodafone asks customers to register it with them.
- **Lock your phone:** the Gardaí advise turning on PIN security and keeping the phone locked at all times.
- **Check Find My or Find Hub is on:** without it, you can't find, lock or erase the phone remotely.
- **Mark it:** the Gardaí also suggest property-marking your phone with your own unique letters or numbers.

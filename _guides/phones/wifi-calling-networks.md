Vodafone, Three and eir all offer WiFi Calling, which lets you make and receive calls over WiFi where your mobile signal is poor. It costs nothing extra: calls come out of your plan's allowance as usual, but your phone has to be on your network's list of supported phones.
{: .answer}

The details differ. eir's WiFi Calling also carries texts and works outside Ireland. Vodafone's doesn't carry texts and isn't available when you're roaming, and Three's works in the Republic of Ireland only.
{: .answer}

## Network by network

| Network | Texts over WiFi | Outside Ireland |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vodafone | No | Not available when roaming |
| Three | Its pages don't say | Republic of Ireland only |
| eir | Yes | Works anywhere; calls to Irish numbers come from your allowance |

Vodafone and eir say you also need an eligible plan, and eir's phone list says you can use it on an eir Bill Pay plan. Three's isn't available with its Mobile Call Recording service.

## What it costs

There's no extra charge from Vodafone, Three or eir. Calls use your plan's minutes, and once those run out, they cost the same as a normal mobile call. Three says it doesn't charge for the data WiFi Calling uses, but whoever provides the WiFi might.

When you use eir's WiFi Calling abroad, calls to Irish numbers come out of your allowance. Calls to numbers in the country you're in, or anywhere else outside Ireland, are charged at eir's standard international rates from Ireland. To pay normal roaming rates for those calls instead, eir says to turn WiFi Calling off.

## Which phones work

Each network publishes a list of phones that work with its WiFi Calling. Vodafone's includes all models launched since July 2022 from the brands it lists, and eir says all Samsung phones in its range support it.

Vodafone says phones bought from other networks or other sellers might not work with its WiFi Calling. If your phone came from abroad, see [Will a phone bought abroad work in Ireland?]({{ '/phones/phone-bought-abroad/' | relative_url }})

Keep your phone's software up to date: Three says you may need an update, and eir needs the latest firmware.

## Turning it on

There's no app to download. Connect to WiFi, then switch WiFi Calling on in your phone's settings.

- **iPhone with current software:** Settings > Apps > Phone > Wi-Fi Calling, then turn on Wi-Fi Calling on This iPhone and choose Enable. On older software, Phone is straight under Settings.
- **Android:** Vodafone's steps are to open the Phone app, tap the menu at the top right, choose Settings and turn Wi-Fi Calling on. Menus differ between phones, and Three's online device guides give the steps for many models.

If you don't see the option, your phone may not support it.

WiFi Calling needs a strong WiFi connection where you are, and Three says a call drops if the WiFi signal goes. If your WiFi doesn't reach every room, see [How to fix Wi-Fi dead spots at home]({{ '/broadband/wifi-dead-spots/' | relative_url }}).

## Emergency calls

If you call the emergency services while using WiFi Calling, Three and eir say your phone tries the mobile network first, and uses WiFi only if there's no mobile signal. eir says that over WiFi, the emergency services won't be able to identify your location, and Three says they may need to check or take your address. So be ready to tell them where you are.

## Other networks

Sky Mobile says its SIM plans support WiFi Calling, depending on your phone. If you're with another network, check its website or ask it before you rely on WiFi Calling.

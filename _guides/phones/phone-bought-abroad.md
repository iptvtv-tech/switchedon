Yes, if it's unlocked and supports Irish networks. A phone locked to a network abroad won't work with an Irish SIM until that network unlocks it, and a phone without 4G Calling on your Irish network may fall back to the older 2G network for calls.
{: .answer}

{% include verdict.html %}

## Check it's unlocked

Vodafone Ireland says a phone locked to another network won't work on its network: you need to contact the network it's locked to and get it unlocked. To check, put in your Irish SIM. Tesco Mobile Ireland says that if it doesn't work or shows an error, the phone is likely locked to another network.

## Check it supports 4G Calling in Ireland

ComReg says Vodafone switched off its 3G network in October 2024, and eir's 3G switch-off ran from 1 October to 6 November 2026. That includes Clear Mobile and An Post, which use Vodafone's network, and GoMo and Lyca Mobile, which use eir's.

4G Calling, also called VoLTE, lets you make calls over the 4G network, and you need a phone that supports it. Vodafone says the phones on its list support 4G Calling when bought from Vodafone, but phones bought from other networks and third parties might not. Three lists the phones that support 4G calling on its network, and says to check your phone maker's website if yours isn't on the list.

If your phone has 4G but not 4G Calling, Vodafone says it will still receive calls over Vodafone's 2G network, though Vodafone recommends a newer phone.

On an iPhone, go to **Settings > Mobile Data > Mobile Data Options > Voice & Data** and choose 5G/4G with VoLTE on. Three says iPhone 12 and later have 4G calling switched on by default.

## Check how it takes a SIM

Apple says iPhone 14 and later models bought in the United States support eSIM only. An unlocked eSIM-only iPhone can be activated with networks around the world, Apple says, but ask your Irish network to confirm it supports eSIM on your plan. For how each Irish network sets up eSIM, see [Switching to eSIM in Ireland: what to check first]({{ '/phones/switch-to-esim/' | relative_url }}).

## Check the frequency bands

ComReg lists the mobile bands assigned in Ireland: 700 MHz, 800 MHz and 900 MHz, generally used to provide coverage, and 1800 MHz, 2.1 GHz, 2.3 GHz, 2.6 GHz and 3.6 GHz, generally used to add capacity. For an iPhone, Apple says to check its band support for the country you'll use it in.

## Bringing it home

- **From another EU country:** Revenue says you won't be charged duty or tax on goods you bring into Ireland if you've already paid them in another EU country.
- **From outside the EU:** you can bring in goods worth up to €430 in total free of duty and tax (€215 if you're under 15). If a single item is worth more than your allowance, you pay duty or tax on its full value, and you should use the red channel at the airport or port.
- **Posted from outside the EU:** the CCPC says you may have to pay extra taxes and charges when it arrives. [Buying tech from a UK shop: VAT, customs and returns]({{ '/buying/uk-shop-vat-customs/' | relative_url }}) explains how that works for UK orders.
- **Your rights:** the CCPC says you have fewer consumer rights when you buy from a business outside the EU than from one in the EU. See [Your rights when tech is faulty in Ireland]({{ '/buying/faulty-tech-rights/' | relative_url }}).

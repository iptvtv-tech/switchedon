Yes. Laptops first put on sale in the EU from 28 April 2026 must have a USB-C charging port, so a USB-C charger from any brand can charge them if it gives enough power. A laptop offered with a charger must also be offered without one, so check whether one is in the box.
{: .answer}

{% include verdict.html %}

## Which laptops it covers

The rules cover any portable computer, and have applied to laptops since 28 April 2026. Phones, tablets, headphones, keyboards, mice and other devices have had to charge by USB-C since 28 December 2024. In Ireland, the rules were brought in by S.I. No. 30 of 2024.

They apply to laptops first put on sale in the EU from that date. Laptops already on sale before then can still be sold, so not every laptop in a shop has to follow them.

Laptops that need more than 240 watts to charge aren't exempt: they must still have a USB-C port, though they can also have their own charging port.

Laptops that charge at more than 15 watts must also support USB Power Delivery (USB PD), the common fast-charging standard for USB-C.

## With or without a charger

A laptop offered with a charger must also be offered without one. But a laptop offered only without a charger doesn't have to be offered with one.

The box shows a pictogram saying whether a charger is included. When you buy online, the pictogram must be shown near the price.

## Will your old charger work?

A label on the box and in the instructions gives two figures in watts: the least power the laptop needs to charge, and the power it needs to charge at full speed. It also says "USB PD" if the laptop supports USB Power Delivery. Online, the label must be shown near the price.

{% include figure.html svg="diagrams/charger-label.svg" alt="The EU power label: a drawing of a charger with XX – YY W and USB PD written on it. USB PD is shown if it supports USB Power Delivery." caption="The EU power label, redrawn from the European Commission's files. The label is there so you can tell whether a USB-C charger you already have meets the laptop's needs. ComReg encourages reusing a compatible charger. ## If a laptop doesn't follow the rules

ComReg, the communications regulator, is the authority in Ireland that checks that radio equipment, such as anything with Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, follows these rules. You can report a product that doesn't comply using the [webform on ComReg's website](https://www.comreg.ie/industry/product-safety/report-a-product-safety-issue/), and ComReg says it will reply within 5 working days.

Check the course's own laptop requirements before you buy. Irish colleges and departments publish them, and they range from UCC's view that most standard Windows laptops are enough to Trinity engineering's minimum of 16GB of memory and a 512GB SSD.
{: .answer}

Engineering courses and UCD's business schools need Windows for some of their software. Students can buy laptops at a discount through the HEAnet Store and Apple's education store.
{: .answer}

{% include key-points.html %}

## Check the course first

Trinity's IT Services advises checking with the course office or website before you buy, as some courses have their own requirements. UCC suggests thinking about your modules too: statistics or data modelling need a higher specification.

Starting engineering at UCD? Its advice for first years is not to buy a new laptop yet if you have one you can use for 30 hours a week, and to wait until second year if you can, when prices have fallen and you know your course's needs. A tablet won't do as your main device.

## What to look for

|  | Light use (UCC) | General coursework (UCC) | Engineering minimum (Trinity) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Memory | 8GB or more | 16GB | 16GB |
| Processor | More than 4 cores | Intel i5 or Core Ultra 5, or AMD Ryzen 5 or 7 | Intel Core i5 or equivalent |
| Storage | 128GB SSD or more | Matters less: most work is in the cloud | 512GB SSD |
| Screen | Reasonable quality | Not specified | 13" or bigger, 1920x1080 or better |
| Battery | Not specified | Not specified | At least 8 hours |
| Ports | Not specified | Not specified | USB-A |

Trinity asks engineering students for at least 8 hours' battery because some tutorial spaces have no power sockets. If a laptop has only USB-C, adapters to USB-A exist, but count their cost.

New laptops don't always come with a charger: see [Do new laptops have to charge by USB-C in Ireland?]({{ '/computers/usb-c-laptop-charging/' | relative_url }})

UCD's business schools ask for at least 8GB of memory, with 16GB or more recommended, an SSD and, ideally, an Ethernet port. Office 365 is available to all UCD students.

## Windows, Mac or Chromebook?

**Windows.** UCC says most standard Windows laptops are enough. Trinity students can buy Windows 11 Home and upgrade it free to Windows 11 Education through the college.

**Mac.** MacBooks cost more than most Windows laptops, but UCC calls them reliable and long-lasting. They're not for engineering at UCD, where most software from second year on won't run on macOS, or for UCD's business schools, which say Apple isn't fully compatible with their online learning environment. Trinity also warns that laptops with ARM-based processors may not work with some of its IT services, and UCD notes that MacBooks from 2020 on are ARM-based.

**Chromebook.** UCC rates Chromebooks for light work, and they fit its Google services, but some apps aren't available. UCC also lends Chromebooks from the Boole Library.

For lighter work, UCC says an iPad with a keyboard could be enough.

## Researched picks

Both picks meet the memory, storage and screen minimums above, checked against Irish maker and retailer pages. We haven't tested them.

## Student discounts

- **HEAnet Store:** college students can get discounts on Windows and Apple laptops; UCC lists Dell and Apple offers there. Trinity students can also order Apple hardware from Select.
- **Apple Store for Education:** education pricing on Macs for college students in Ireland, after verification.
- **Microsoft Store Ireland:** students from primary school to college, their parents and teaching staff get up to 10% off selected Surface devices, PCs and accessories online.

## Warranty and your rights

UCC suggests keeping some budget for extra warranty and accidental damage cover. UCD engineering recommends at least a 1-year warranty, and an extended one if you can.

If a laptop turns out to be faulty, see [Your rights when tech is faulty in Ireland]({{ '/buying/faulty-tech-rights/' | relative_url }}). Ordering from a UK website? [Buying tech from a UK shop]({{ '/buying/uk-shop-vat-customs/' | relative_url }}) explains the extra charges.

## If a secondary school asks you to buy a device

The CCPC's guidance to schools says they should:

- not require parents to buy non-essential items as part of a technology bundle, and let parents get insurance and accessories separately
- appoint an exclusive supplier only after a competitive process
- if they don't use the public procurement frameworks, consider letting parents buy their own device from any shop, as long as it meets the minimum requirements, refurbished devices included
- consult parents and consider the cost before bringing in devices.

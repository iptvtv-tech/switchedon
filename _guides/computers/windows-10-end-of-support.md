Windows 10 no longer gets regular updates, but you can still get its security updates free until 12 October 2027. In Ireland and the rest of the European Economic Area, enrol in Microsoft's Extended Security Updates and keep signing in to the PC with your Microsoft account.
{: .answer}

If your PC meets Windows 11's requirements, the upgrade is free. If it doesn't, Microsoft's advice is to enrol in those updates or replace the PC.
{: .answer}

## What end of support means

Support for Windows 10 ended on 14 October 2025. Your PC keeps working, but Microsoft no longer sends it new features, security fixes or technical support, which makes it more vulnerable to viruses and malware.

If you use Microsoft 365 apps, they keep working on Windows 10 and get security updates until 10 October 2028. Microsoft still recommends Windows 11 to avoid performance and reliability problems over time.

## Can your PC run Windows 11?

The upgrade from Windows 10 Home or Pro to the same edition of Windows 11 is free. To see if your PC can take it, go to **Start > Settings > Update & Security > Windows Update** and select **Check for updates**: if your PC meets the requirements, the upgrade is offered there. Your PC needs to be on Windows 10 version 22H2. If your internet use is metered, your provider's charges may apply to the download.

Windows 11 needs at least:

| Part | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Processor | 1GHz or faster, with 2 or more cores, on a compatible 64-bit processor |
| Memory | 4GB |
| Storage | 64GB |
| Firmware | UEFI, Secure Boot capable |
| TPM (Trusted Platform Module) | Version 2.0 |
| Graphics | DirectX 12 or later, with a WDDM 2.0 driver |
| Display | 720p, bigger than 9 inches |

If you're not sure, Microsoft's free PC Health Check app checks your PC (though not its graphics card or display), or you can ask your PC's maker. Windows 11 Home and Windows 11 Pro for personal use need an internet connection and a Microsoft account when you first set them up.

## Free security updates until October 2027

Microsoft's Extended Security Updates (ESU) programme sends critical and important security updates to Windows 10 PCs until 12 October 2027. It doesn't add features, fix other problems or come with technical support. You can enrol any time before then, and if you've already enrolled, you're covered to that date without doing anything.

For consumers in the European Economic Area, there are two ways in:

- **Free:** stay signed in to the PC with your Microsoft account. If you stop signing in with it, updates stop after up to 60 days and you'll need to enrol again.
- **One-off payment:** to keep using a local account, pay US$30 or the local equivalent, plus tax. You only need a Microsoft account to make the purchase.

One ESU licence covers up to 10 PCs.

To enrol:

1. Make sure the PC runs Windows 10 version 22H2 (Home, Pro, Pro Education or Workstations) with the latest Windows update installed.
2. Sign in with an administrator account. The Microsoft account you use can't be a child account.
3. Go to **Settings > Update & Security > Windows Update**. If the PC is eligible, you'll see a link to enrol.
4. Select it and follow the steps. If you use a local account, you'll be asked to sign in with a Microsoft account.

## If you're replacing the PC

For a PC that can't run Windows 11, Microsoft's advice is to enrol in ESU or replace it with one that can. Windows Backup can save your files, settings and apps from the old PC and transfer them to the new one. Moving more than 5GB needs a Microsoft subscription or trial, and some apps have to be reinstalled from their makers.

## Getting rid of the old PC safely

Before you recycle, sell or give away a Windows 10 PC, Microsoft's steps are:

1. Back up anything you want to keep.
2. Go to **Settings > Update & Security > Recovery** and reset the PC. Choose **Remove everything**, which reinstalls Windows and removes your files, apps and settings, and turn on **Clean data**, which cleans the drive so removed files are harder to recover. If the PC is encrypted, you'll need your BitLocker recovery key.
3. Remove the PC from your Microsoft account at account.microsoft.com/devices.

Microsoft notes that Clean data doesn't meet government and industry data-erasure standards.

Recycling the PC is free. Bring it to your local recycling centre or civic amenity site, or to a shop when you buy a new one, as long as that shop sells the same type of product. It doesn't have to be the shop where you bought the old PC.

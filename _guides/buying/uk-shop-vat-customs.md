If you order from a shop in Great Britain, you'll pay Irish VAT and customs duty on top of the price. The seller or delivery company can also pass on a €2 handling fee for each different item. Duty is €3 per item on parcels worth €150 or less, and the normal duty for those goods above that.
{: .answer}

You pay these at checkout, or to the delivery company before it hands over your parcel. The handling fee and the €3 duty aren't refunded if you change your mind and send it back. Goods that are in Northern Ireland when you order them carry no customs duty or handling fee.
{: .answer}

## What you'll pay on top of the price

Customs duty and VAT are due on everything you buy online from outside the EU, including Great Britain, whatever it's worth.

{% include price-date.html what="Charges" note="They can change, so check Revenue's and An Post's own pages before you order." %}

| Charge | Parcel worth €150 or less | Parcel worth more than €150 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VAT | The Irish rate for those goods | The Irish rate for those goods |
| Customs duty | €3 per item | The normal duty for those goods |
| Handling fee | €2 per item | €2 per item |

"Per item" means each different product. A phone case, a charger and a cable in one parcel count as three items: €9 in duty, plus VAT. Two identical cables count as one item.

The €3 duty has applied since 1 July 2026, and the handling fee since 1 November 2026. The seller or the delivery company pays the handling fee, and they can pass it on to you.

For parcels worth more than €150, duty is worked out on the price plus postage and insurance.

## Check before you buy

- **Where the shop really is.** A .ie web address, prices in euro or the website's name don't prove a shop is in the EU. Its terms and conditions, privacy policy, "about us" or "contact us" page should say where the business is and where the goods come from.
- **The checkout total.** Check whether it includes Irish VAT and customs duty. If the site says "taxes and duties included" or shows import charges at checkout, nothing more should be due on delivery. If it isn't clear, assume VAT and duty may be due when the parcel arrives.
- **The shop's policies.** Read its policy on customs and taxes, and its returns terms: whether you can return the item, whether you'd post it abroad at your own cost, and whether you can cancel before it's dispatched.

## If you're asked to pay on delivery

If the shop didn't collect the charges at checkout, the delivery company will ask you to pay the duty and handling fee before it delivers. You'll pay VAT then too if the shop didn't charge it.

If An Post is delivering:

- You'll get a postcard with the charges, and an email or text as well if the sender gave An Post your details.
- An Post may add a customs administration fee of €6.95. It lists the handling fee as a minimum of €2 plus VAT per item.
- There's an extra €10 charge if VAT is due, wasn't charged at checkout and the sender's electronic details were incomplete.
- Pay online by card with the customs reference number from the postcard, email or text, or at any post office by card or cash. You can't pay by phone.
- Pay by the "Pay by Date" on the postcard, or the parcel goes back to the sender. You can also refuse the parcel online or at a post office.

Watch out for fake texts and emails asking for customs payments. An Post never puts a link in a message about customs charges, and Revenue never texts or emails asking for them. Revenue's advice is not to click any links and to delete the message.

## Sending it back

If you change your mind and return something, you won't get back:

- the €2 handling fee
- the €3 duty on a parcel worth €150 or less
- An Post's administration fee.

Whether you get the VAT back is up to the shop: some refund it when you return an item, but many don't.

Revenue says duty on parcels worth more than €150 may be refunded if you return the goods within 90 days. You claim through the courier or postal company that dealt with customs for your parcel, as Revenue doesn't pay refunds to consumers directly. But An Post says it doesn't refund VAT or customs charges on goods returned after delivery. Check with the delivery company before you send it back.

## If it's faulty

When you buy from outside the EU, you have fewer consumer rights than when you buy from a business in the EU, Iceland or Norway.

1. Contact the shop and follow its complaints process.
2. If it doesn't respond or won't help, and you paid by debit or credit card, you may be able to ask your bank for a chargeback. In most cases you have 90 to 120 days to dispute a payment.
3. You can't use the small claims procedure: the Irish and European procedures don't cover the UK, including Northern Ireland. You may need independent legal advice instead.

The €3 duty may be refunded if the goods are faulty: contact the shop first, then the courier or postal company that dealt with customs. The €2 handling fee isn't refunded, even for faulty goods.

If you bought from a shop in Ireland or elsewhere in the EU, you have more rights: see [Your rights when tech is faulty in Ireland]({{ '/buying/faulty-tech-rights/' | relative_url }}).

## Northern Ireland and EU shops

Goods that are in Northern Ireland, Ireland or another EU country when you order them carry no customs duty or handling fee. There are no customs declarations or charges on goods moving between Northern Ireland and Ireland.

What counts is where the goods are: goods sent from Great Britain carry duty, VAT and the handling fee. The small claims procedure doesn't cover Northern Ireland either.

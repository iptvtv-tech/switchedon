Recycling an old phone, laptop or TV is free in Ireland: bring it to any local authority recycling centre, or to a shop when you buy a new one of a similar type. Large electrical shops must also take small items, under 25cm, without you buying anything.
{: .answer}

Before you hand in a phone or laptop, back it up and erase it, using the maker's own steps.
{: .answer}

## Recycling centres

Any local authority recycling centre, also called a civic amenity site, takes household electrical items free of charge. That covers anything with a plug, battery or cable, including mobile phones and computers.

To find your nearest one, use the drop-off finder on [mywaste.ie](https://mywaste.ie/sustainability/weee/help-faqs/) or WEEE Ireland's [map of free drop-off points](https://www.weeeireland.ie/household-recycling/where-can-i-recycle/).

It's free because a small recycling charge, the vEMC, is added to the price of certain new products and pays for collecting and recycling them.

## Taking it back to a shop

When you buy a new item, the shop must take back your old one free of charge, one for one. The old item must be of a similar type or have done the same job as the new one: an old TV when you buy a TV, for example.

Shops with more than 400 square metres of space for selling electrical goods must also take small items, less than 25cm, without you buying anything.

Some shops take back household items with no purchase needed at all. WEEE Ireland's map includes participating electrical retailers.

Shops that sell online have to provide take-back too.

## Getting a new TV or other item delivered

If a shop delivers a new item, you're entitled to have the old one collected at the same time, at no extra cost, as long as it's a similar type. The shop can charge for delivery, but not for collecting the old one.

The shop must give you 24 hours' notice of delivery, and the old item must be ready: clean, empty and disconnected. If you weren't given notice and it wasn't ready, the shop must come back for it within 15 days.

You can also bring the old item back to the shop yourself at any time, or to a recycling centre.

## Free collection days

In areas without easy access to a recycling centre, WEEE Ireland holds free collection events. You can drop off any household item with a plug or battery.

WEEE Ireland doesn't run household electrical recycling in Kerry, Clare, Monaghan, Cavan, Louth, Meath or the Fingal County Council area. If you live there, look up drop-off points on mywaste.ie. ERP Ireland runs free drop-off events in some of these counties, and electrical shops and recycling centres there take items free too.

## Erase your phone or laptop first

Back up anything you want to keep, then erase the device. Each maker publishes its own steps.

- **iPhone or iPad:** back up and sign out of your Apple Account. Then go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone and tap Erase All Content and Settings. If it uses an eSIM, choose to erase the eSIM profile too. Erasing turns off Find My and Activation Lock, but doesn't delete what's stored in iCloud.
- **Android phone:** Google says a factory reset erases all your data from your phone. First make sure your data is backed up to your Google Account, that you know the Google Account on the phone, and that it's charged to at least 70%. On most phones you reset it in the Settings app; your phone maker's support site has the exact steps.
- **Mac:** back up, then use Erase All Content and Settings to reset it to factory settings. Macs without that option are erased from macOS Recovery.
- **Windows 10 PC:** follow Microsoft's steps in our guide to [Windows 10 support ending]({{ '/computers/windows-10-end-of-support/' | relative_url }}#getting-rid-of-the-old-pc-safely).

## Apple devices

Apple's Irish site says that if an Apple device isn't eligible for its Trade In scheme, Apple will recycle it for free, whatever its model or condition, through [apple.com/ie](https://www.apple.com/ie/trade-in/). Back up and remove your personal data first.

## Never put it in the bin

Don't put old electrical items in your household bin. Lithium-ion batteries, and devices that run on them, can be crushed or damaged in bins, which can cause fires in bin lorries, waste facilities and recycling centres.

Loose batteries go in the red or blue battery boxes in many supermarkets and shops. You don't have to buy anything: shops that sell batteries must take old ones back free.

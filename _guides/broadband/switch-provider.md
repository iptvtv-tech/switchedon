Ask your new provider to switch you. It manages the move, so you don't cancel or give your old provider 30 days' notice, and you shouldn't be without broadband for more than one working day.
{: .answer}

Switching itself is free. If you're still in your minimum contract term, though, your old provider may charge an early termination fee.
{: .answer}

## How a switch works

Under ComReg's rules, your new provider manages the switch, and you don't need to tell your old provider.

{% include figure.html svg="diagrams/broadband-switch-steps.svg" alt="Timeline of a broadband switch. Step 1, you: tell the new provider you want to switch, with your account number and UAN ready. Step 2, the new provider: it keeps you updated, and you can agree a date for the switch; you do not need to tell your old provider. Step 3, the new provider, on switch day: it checks your new service works, then tells your old provider to stop the old one. Step 4, the old provider: your old contract ends when the new service starts, you get a final bill, and overpayments are refunded. You should not be without broadband for more than one working day." caption="Who does what when you switch broadband, based on ComReg's pages listed in Sources." %}

1. Have your details ready. You need to be the account holder. Have your name, address or Eircode, your current provider's name, your account number and the Universal Account Number (UAN) from your bill; account numbers are on your bill or app.
2. When you order from the new provider, tell it you want to switch. If you're adding a new connection and keeping your current one, say so instead.
3. You can agree a date for the switch. The new provider should keep you updated.
4. On switch day, the new provider checks your new service is working, then tells your old provider to stop the old one. Your old service isn't cancelled before then.

If there's a problem, you shouldn't be without service for more than one working day, and you should be kept updated.

There's no overlap or double billing, because your old contract ends when the new service starts. Your old provider sends a final bill and refunds any overpayment automatically.

If the new service needs installing, for example a move to fibre, arrange that with your new provider.

## What it costs

Switching is free. If you leave during your minimum contract term, your old provider may charge an early termination fee, usually the same as what you'd have paid for the rest of the contract.

Keeping your landline number is free, but tell your new provider you want to keep it: it isn't moved automatically. For your mobile number, see [How to keep your number when you switch mobile network]({{ '/phones/keep-your-number/' | relative_url }}).

Many providers ask you to return equipment such as the router when your contract ends, and may charge you if you don't. If you keep it, the charge can't be more than the lower of its depreciated value, which is listed in your contract, or the remaining service fee.

## When to switch

ComReg says the best time is when you're nearing the end of your minimum contract term. Your provider must tell you when the term is ending, that you can cancel and how, and send you its best tariff for your needs before the term ends.

Once you're outside the minimum term, you can switch any time. Many contracts carry on at the same terms after they expire, and any new agreement may start a new minimum term.

Watch for price rises:

- If your contract has a built-in annual price increase, you generally can't leave without a penalty during the minimum term. These increases usually happen in April; if you're outside the minimum term, ComReg suggests switching before they take effect.
- If your contract has no built-in increase, your provider must give you at least one month's notice of any change to it and tell you, where it applies, about your right to leave without penalty. A change like that can let you switch without a fee, even within your minimum term.

## Before you sign with the new provider

You should get a Contract Summary, setting out the main terms in plain language, before you sign. No provider can put you on a minimum term longer than 24 months.

If you sign up online or by phone, you can cancel within 14 days; a door-to-door sale gives you 30 days. There's no cooling-off period if you sign up in a shop.

If your phone service is moving from a traditional landline to an internet (VoIP) line, check with the new provider about home security or telecare alarms, as the change may affect them.

[ComReg Compare](https://www.comreg.ie/compare) helps you find a plan, and ComReg's [Broadband Checker](https://www.comreg.ie/broadbandchecker) shows who provides broadband in your area. For more on checking your address, see [Can I get fibre broadband at my address?]({{ '/broadband/fibre-at-my-address/' | relative_url }}).

## Moving house

Moving during your contract? Ask your provider about options other than paying an early termination fee, such as moving the service to your new address. It doesn't have to agree.

## If something goes wrong

Providers must compensate you for loss of service, switching delays, delays in ending your old service and not keeping you informed. Each provider publishes its compensation scheme, including how to claim, on its website.

To complain:

1. Complain to your provider under its code of practice, and get a complaint reference number.
2. The provider must respond within 10 working days.
3. If it doesn't acknowledge your complaint or respond in time, or you're unhappy with its answer, contact ComReg's Consumer Care team, which may take it up with the provider for you: (01) 804 9668, Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm and Saturday 9am to 1pm, or consumerline@comreg.ie.

Your complaint to the provider must have been made within the previous 12 months.

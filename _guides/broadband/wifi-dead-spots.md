Where your router sits makes a big difference to your Wi-Fi: the further you are from it, and the more walls in between, the weaker the signal. Start with simple fixes, like moving the router somewhere central and using a cable where you can.
{: .answer}

If that's not enough, Virgin Media, Vodafone, eir and Sky each offer Wi-Fi pods or boosters that work with their own router.
{: .answer}

{% include key-points.html %}

## First, test your speed over a cable

If your broadband is giving trouble, ComReg suggests connecting to your router with an ethernet cable, checking what speed you get, and comparing it with the package you pay for. Speeds may be noticeably slower at the busiest times of the day.

If your speed over a cable is fine but some rooms are slow, start with the fixes below. If it's poor even over a cable, go to [If your broadband itself is slow](#if-your-broadband-itself-is-slow).

## Move the router

ComReg says your router's position will greatly influence which parts of your home get the best connection. If it's in an upstairs room, for example, its signal may not reach a room in the opposite corner of the house.

National Broadband Ireland (NBI) gives these tips on where to put it:

- **In the middle of your home.** Routers send Wi-Fi out in a circle, so on a windowsill or against a wall much of the signal goes to waste.
- **Out in the open,** not behind a sofa or in a cupboard.
- **Up off the floor,** which absorbs much of the signal. Try the top of a desk or sideboard, or a high shelf, where it can reach further and over obstacles.
- **Away from large appliances,** especially microwaves, which use the same 2.4GHz band as Wi-Fi and can interfere with it.
- **Away from water and shiny surfaces.** Water, such as a fish tank, can limit Wi-Fi, and mirrors and other flat, shiny surfaces reflect the signal.
- **With its aerials pointing up,** if your router has aerials that stick up.

## Cut down what's competing for your Wi-Fi

- **How many devices are on.** The more devices connected, the more chance that too many are using the internet at once, which can slow your speeds. Uploading or downloading video is especially demanding. ComReg notes that we often have more devices connected than we realise, such as smart doorbells, security systems, smart home devices and smart appliances.
- **Your Wi-Fi channel.** Many routers pick a channel automatically when first set up, and a nearby Wi-Fi network may already be using it. ComReg says a different channel may give better speeds, and it could be worth experimenting to find what works best in your home.
- **Restarts and updates.** ComReg advises restarting your devices often and keeping software and apps up to date. If one older device is slow everywhere, ComReg notes that older hardware can struggle to run newer software.

## Use a cable where you can

ComReg says a wired ethernet connection will always be more reliable than Wi-Fi and will generally be faster. That means connecting a device, such as a computer or laptop, directly to the router with an ethernet cable.

## Add a booster or mesh system

ComReg says Wi-Fi boosters, also called range extenders, improve how far your Wi-Fi signal reaches. NBI suggests a range extender or a mesh system if nothing else works: an extender picks up your router's signal and sends it on to your devices, while a mesh system uses several units to spread Wi-Fi across your home.

Before you buy one, check what your provider offers. Each of these works with that provider's own router:

- **Virgin Media:** the WiFi Guarantee gives you a Smart Wi-Fi Pod, plus up to 2 more, with a guaranteed 30Mb in every room or €150 credit. It costs €5 a month, or is free with 1Gb or 2Gb broadband.
- **Vodafone:** Super Wi-Fi adds mesh extenders to the Vodafone Gigabox modem, installed by an engineer, for €5 a month.
- **eir:** its Wi-Fi boosters are plug and play, from €4.99 a month per booster on a zero-month contract. The WiFi 7 booster is only for eir's 5Gb package; customers on slower packages can get the WiFi 6 booster.
- **Sky:** WiFi Max uses Sky Max Pods, which Sky lends you, and promises guaranteed speeds in every room or your money back. It's free with Gigafast+ speeds; Sky's page doesn't list a separate price.

## If your broadband itself is slow

Your provider has to include the minimum, normally available, maximum and advertised download and upload speeds in your contract. If your speed over a cable falls short of what you were promised, ComReg's advice on complaining is:

- Check what the provider promised on speeds in your contract, Contract Summary, welcome email or online account.
- Keep evidence: speed tests, bills, screenshots, copies of emails and chats, and notes of calls (date, time, who you spoke to and what they said).
- Ask for a complaint reference number. Providers must acknowledge a complaint within two working days, and must send you a response within 10 working days. Every provider must have a complaints code of practice that's easy to find on its home page.
- If it doesn't respond in time, or you're unhappy with its answer, ComReg's Consumer Care team may be able to take it up with your provider for you, as long as you complained to the provider within the previous 12 months. Call (01) 804 9668 (Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm, Saturday 9am to 1pm) or email consumerline@comreg.ie.

5G home broadband connects your home to the internet through a mobile network instead of a cable: a router with a SIM card picks up the 5G signal and shares it over Wi-Fi. Vodafone, Three and eir all sell it with unlimited data, but ComReg says mobile broadband won't normally be as fast or as reliable as fixed broadband, so check what fibre you can get, and the 5G signal at your address, before you sign up.
{: .answer}

Vodafone says it may suit you if you want a quick setup, have no fixed phone line or expect to move house. Vodafone and Three let you send it back if it doesn't work well at your home.
{: .answer}

## How it works

ComReg describes mobile broadband as a Wi-Fi router with a SIM card that connects to the same 5G or 4G signals as a mobile phone. Several devices can connect to it at once, as with fixed broadband.

There's usually no phone line, cable, drilling or engineer visit. The router is delivered to you, and you plug it in near a window or wherever the signal is strongest. Vodafone says its router arrives in 1 to 2 working days; eir says to allow 3 to 4 working days.

Three also offers a technician install: an engineer fits a modem on an outside wall, sets up the router indoors and lines everything up for the strongest 5G signal. Three says this suits homes where the 5G signal indoors is weaker. Its self-install option works best with a strong indoor signal.

5G home broadband isn't the same as fixed wireless broadband, which ComReg describes as radio signals from a local base station to a receiver at your home.

## Check fibre first

ComReg says mobile broadband won't normally be as fast or as reliable as fixed internet. Before you choose 5G, put your Eircode into ComReg's Broadband Checker to see what fixed broadband is available or planned at your home: see [Can I get fibre broadband at my address?]({{ '/broadband/fibre-at-my-address/' | relative_url }}).

## Check the signal at your address

ComReg's advice is to check the mobile coverage in your area and compare providers' 4G and 5G signal. Building materials and insulation can weaken the signal indoors. For why, and what helps, see [Poor mobile signal at home? Your options in Ireland]({{ '/phones/poor-mobile-signal/' | relative_url }}).

Each provider lets you check your address:

- **Vodafone** checks the coverage at your address by chat. It says your speed will depend on the 5G coverage and network conditions there.
- **Three** has a coverage checker, and its plans are subject to an address check. It says speeds may vary.
- **eir** has a coverage map you can search by Eircode or address.

Your contract must state the estimated maximum and advertised download and upload speeds, so read those before you sign.

All of these come with unlimited data. Vodafone's router is free, and so is eir's on the 12-month plan.

For Three's prices for new customers, see its broadband page.

Prices rise every April: by €3 a month at Vodafone and Three, and by €2.50 a month at eir from April 2027.

Vodafone's plans are for use in the Republic of Ireland only, and don't include data roaming. Three's unlimited data is for use in Ireland.

## If it doesn't work well at your home

- **Vodafone:** you can return the router within 14 days if the service isn't suitable for your home.
- **Three:** it offers a 30-day money-back guarantee, and you can return the equipment free.

If you move house, Vodafone says you can take the router with you anywhere in the Republic of Ireland, but check the coverage at your new address first.

If neither fibre nor 5G works where you live, see [Does Starlink work in Ireland?]({{ '/broadband/starlink-ireland/' | relative_url }}). To move from your current provider, see [How to switch broadband provider in Ireland]({{ '/broadband/switch-provider/' | relative_url }}).

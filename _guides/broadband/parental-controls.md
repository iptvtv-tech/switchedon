The simplest place to start is your broadband provider's parental controls, which cover every device on your home Wi-Fi: Virgin Media's and Sky's cost nothing extra, Vodafone's costs €2.99 a month, and eir's modem lets you set time limits. On your child's phone, also use Screen Time on an iPhone or Google Family Link on Android, because a broadband filter covers your home Wi-Fi, not mobile data.
{: .answer}

No filter catches everything. Webwise, which Coimisiún na Meán points parents to, says parental controls are not 100% effective, and that talking to your child about using the internet safely matters too.
{: .answer}

## 1. Your broadband provider's controls

{% include price-date.html note="They can change, so check the provider's own page before you sign up." %}

Webwise says setting parental controls through your internet provider can be the easiest way to manage what your children can access on all the devices in your home.

- **Virgin Media:** its Parental Controls are a free service for broadband customers. They block websites in categories such as adult content, nudity, drugs, hate and racism, violence, hacking and weapons, on any device connected to your home broadband. Sign in to My Virgin Media to set age-appropriate filters. In the My Virgin Media app you can also pause Wi-Fi for particular devices.
- **Sky:** Sky Broadband Shield is available to all Sky broadband customers at no extra cost, and works automatically on every device on your home Wi-Fi. You can't set it differently for each device, but you can change its age rating and schedule by signing in to your Sky account.
- **Vodafone:** Secure Net Home costs €2.99 a month and covers every device on your Vodafone home broadband Wi-Fi. Its parental controls include content filtering, Pause, Bedtime and Focus. You need a Gigabox (VOX 3.0) or Huawei HG659 modem, and you manage it at securenet.vodafone.ie.
- **eir:** eir's Smart Start advice is to use its modem's parental controls to set time limits for the devices in your home, and it has a step-by-step guide for its modem.

## 2. On your child's phone or tablet

Broadband controls only cover your home Wi-Fi. On mobile data, what counts is the controls on the phone itself and any from its mobile network.

**iPhone or iPad.** Apple's parental controls work through Family Sharing, so set that up first: your child needs their own Apple Account in your family group. Then, in Screen Time:

- **Content & Privacy Restrictions** let you manage apps, content and settings by age. Changes need your Screen Time passcode.
- **Ask to Buy** sends you a request to approve or decline before your child buys anything.
- **Limit Adult Websites** is on by default for children under 18. Ask to Browse goes further and shows only websites you approve.

**Android.** Google Family Link lets you set a bedtime and daily screen limits, decide which apps your child can download or buy, restrict mature content on Google Play, and see where their Android phone is. You can use it to create a Google Account for a child under 13, or under the age that applies in your country. You need to be 18 or over and live in the same country as your child.

You can run the Family Link app on an iPhone, but Family Link can only supervise a child's Android phone or tablet, Chromebook or some Fitbit devices, not an iPhone or iPad.

## 3. Your child's mobile network

eir and other Irish mobile networks follow a code of practice that covers parental controls for children's use of mobile services, so ask your child's network what it offers.

- **Three:** its adult content filter blocks content that's unsuitable for children. To add it to an account, message Three's customer care or call 1913. If you're over 18, you can ask to have it removed the same way.
- **Vodafone:** Secure Net Mobile costs €1.99 a month per line. On its own it has no parental controls, but if you have Secure Net Home and your child's line has Secure Net Mobile, the home controls also work on their mobile data.

## 4. Controls in apps: the Online Safety Code

Under Ireland's Online Safety Code, video-sharing platforms based in Ireland whose terms allow under-16s must have parental controls. These protect children from age-inappropriate content, such as pornography or explicit violence, and Coimisiún na Meán says they should be easy to find and easy to use. Its [parental controls page](https://www.cnam.ie/general-public/guides-resources/for-parents/parental-controls/) links to the controls on YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, X, Pinterest and TikTok.

If a platform's controls are hard to find or use, complain to the platform first. If you're not happy with its answer, contact Coimisiún na Meán on +353 1 963 7755 (Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm) or at usersupport@cnam.ie.
